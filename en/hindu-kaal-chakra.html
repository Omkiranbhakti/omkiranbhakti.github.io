Hindu Kaal Chakra — the Time Machine of Sanatan Dharma

152 events from creation to the Kalki avatar — in the order of the yugas, with pictures. Spin the 3D wheel and travel through Sanatan history.

🕉️ Open the 3D Kaal Chakra

The Hindu Kaal Chakra — the time machine of Sanatan Dharma. Travel from the beginning of creation through Shri Ram, Shri Krishna, the Mahabharata, the Gita, the saints and the tirthas, all the way to the Kalki avatar and the return of Satya Yuga — on a turning 3D wheel, with pictures.

In the Sanatan tradition time moves in circles. One mahayuga holds four yugas — Satya, Treta, Dvapara and Kali Yuga. A thousand mahayugas make one day of Lord Brahma, a kalpa, and each kalpa has fourteen Manus. Our kalpa is the Shveta Varaha Kalpa, the manvantara is the Vaivasvata, and the Kali Yuga of the 28th mahayuga is running now — the very words spoken in the sankalpa of every puja.

Below, the events of each yuga are listed in order. Tap ‘See it on the Kaal Chakra’ on any event and the 3D wheel stops right on it. Holy names are written in Roman letters.

In the order of the yugas

  • Creation, Kalpa and Manvantara (13)
  • Satya Yuga (3)
  • Treta Yuga (15)
  • Dvapara Yuga (21)
  • Kali Yuga (86)
  • You are here — the prophecies ahead (the future of Kali Yuga and Kalki) (14)

Creation, Kalpa and Manvantara

  • Beginning of the Shveta Varaha Kalpa

    Ancient (Puranic age) · The cosmos

    In the Puranas one kalpa is a single day of Lord Brahma — 4.32 billion years. The present kalpa is named after Lord Varaha, and we live in its seventh manvantara.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Varaha Avatar — Earth Rescued

    Ancient (Puranic age) · The cosmos

    Hiranyaksha dragged the Earth down to Rasatala. Lord Vishnu took the form of a boar, slew him, and lifted Bhudevi back into place on his tusks.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Svayambhuva Manu — the First Manu

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Brahmavarta

    Born of Lord Brahma, the first Manu and his wife Shatarupa began the human race. Uttanapada and Priyavrata were their sons.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Dhruva's Penance — the Pole Star

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Madhuvan

    Hurt by his stepmother's insult, the boy Dhruva did fierce penance in Madhuvan. Pleased, Narayana gave him an unmoving place in the sky — today's Pole Star.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Daksha's Yajna and Sati's Sacrifice

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Kankhal

    When Daksha insulted Mahadev at his yajna, Sati gave up her body in yogic fire. Virabhadra destroyed the yajna; wherever Sati's body fell, a Shakti Peeth arose.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • King Prithu Milks the Earth

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Brahmavarta

    When the Earth hid her grain, King Prithu milked her in the form of a cow to bring back food and herbs. The Earth is called "Prithvi" after him.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Samudra Manthan and the Kurma Avatar

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Kshira Sagar (Ocean of Milk)

    Devas and asuras churned the Ocean of Milk with Mount Mandara as the rod and Vasuki as the rope. Lord Kurma held the mountain on his back, and fourteen treasures came out.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Mahadev Becomes Neelkanth

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Kshira Sagar (Ocean of Milk)

    The Halahala poison from the churning began to burn all creation. Mahadev drank it and held it in his throat, and was called Neelkanth, the blue-throated one.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Mohini Avatar and the Story of Kumbh

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Prayag, Haridwar, Ujjain, Nashik

    As Mohini, Vishnu served the amrit to the devas. The four places where drops from the amrit pot fell are where the Kumbh Mela is held today.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Beginning of the Vaivasvata Manvantara

    Ancient (Puranic age) · The cosmos

    The age of the seventh Manu, Vaivasvata Manu, began. This is the present manvantara.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Matsya Avatar and the Great Flood

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Himalaya

    In the waters of the pralaya, Lord Matsya tied Manu's boat to his horn and saved it, protecting the Vedas and the Saptarishis.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Ikshvaku and the Solar Dynasty

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Ayodhya

    Ikshvaku, son of Vaivasvata Manu, became the first king of Ayodhya. Harishchandra, Bhagiratha and Shri Ram were later born in this Suryavansh.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Pururava and the Lunar Dynasty

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Pratishthana (Prayag)

    The Chandravansh began with Pururava, son of Budha and Ila. Yadu, Kuru, the Pandavas and Shri Krishna were born in this line.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

Satya Yuga

  • Beginning of Satya Yuga

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Bharatvarsha

    The first yuga of the present, 28th mahayuga. Dharma stood on all four legs — truth, austerity, compassion and charity.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Narasimha Avatar — Prahlada Saved

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Ahobilam (tradition)

    Hiranyakashipu tried to kill his devotee son Prahlada. The Lord burst out of a pillar as Narasimha and slew him on the threshold at dusk.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • King Harishchandra's Test of Truth

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Ayodhya, Kashi

    To keep his word, Harishchandra gave up his kingdom, wife and son and became a servant at the cremation ground. Unshaken in truth, he became immortal.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

Treta Yuga

  • Beginning of Treta Yuga

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Bharatvarsha

    Dharma now stood on three legs. This is the age of the Vamana, Parashurama and Shri Ram avatars.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Vamana Avatar and King Bali

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Bhrigukachchha (tradition)

    Vamana asked the generous King Bali for three steps of land and measured all the worlds in two. Bali offered his head for the third step and became king of Patala.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Bhagiratha Brings Ganga to Earth

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Gangotri

    To free his ancestors, Bhagiratha did hard penance. Mahadev caught Ganga's force in his matted hair, and Mother Ganga came down to Earth.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Vishwamitra Becomes a Brahmarshi

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Kanyakubja

    Defeated by Vasishtha, King Kaushika did penance for thousands of years. In the end Vasishtha himself called him Brahmarshi — he is the seer of the Gayatri Mantra.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Parashurama Avatar

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Narmada, Konkan

    Parashurama, son of Jamadagni, destroyed the tyrant kings. Tradition says he raised the land of Konkan and Kerala from the sea.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Sage Agastya Crosses the Vindhyas

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Vindhya, Podhigai

    Sage Agastya asked the ever-rising Vindhya mountain to bow and went south. He is honoured as the first teacher of South India's rishi tradition.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Birth of Shri Ram

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Ayodhya

    On Chaitra Shukla Navami, Lord Vishnu was born as Shri Ram to King Dasharatha and Mother Kaushalya. Ram Navami is celebrated on this day.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Sita Swayamvar

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Mithila

    King Janaka's condition was to string Shiva's bow. When Shri Ram lifted it, the bow broke, and Mother Sita placed the wedding garland on him.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Shri Ram's Exile

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Ayodhya, Chitrakoot

    Kaikeyi's two boons sent Ram into exile for fourteen years. Sita and Lakshmana went with him; Bharata ruled with Ram's sandals on the throne.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Sita Taken by Ravana

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Panchavati (Nashik)

    Using the trick of the golden deer to draw Ram and Lakshmana away, Ravana carried off Sita. Jatayu tried to stop him and died a hero's death.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Hanuman's Leap to Lanka

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Lanka

    Hanuman leapt across the ocean, gave Ram's ring to Mother Sita in the Ashoka Vatika, and set Lanka on fire.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Building of Ram Setu

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Rameswaram

    Led by Nala and Nila, the vanara army built a bridge over the sea with stones marked with Ram's name. At Rameswaram, Shri Ram installed a Shivalinga.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • The End of Ravana

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Lanka

    After a fierce war, Shri Ram killed Ravana. This victory of dharma over adharma is celebrated on Dussehra.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Shri Ram's Return and Ram Rajya

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Ayodhya

    When Shri Ram returned to Ayodhya after the exile, the people lit lamps — the tradition of Deepavali. His reign is remembered as the ideal Ram Rajya.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Valmiki Composes the Ramayana

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Tamasa river

    Grief at a krauncha bird's death brought the first shloka from the lips of Adikavi Valmiki. He went on to compose the Ramayana of 24,000 shlokas.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

Dvapara Yuga

  • Beginning of Dvapara Yuga

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Bharatvarsha

    Dharma now stood on two legs. This is the age of Shri Krishna and the Mahabharata.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Emperor Bharata — the Name "Bharat"

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Hastinapur region

    Emperor Bharata was the son of Dushyanta and Shakuntala. By tradition the land is called "Bharatvarsha" after him.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • King Kuru and Kurukshetra

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Kurukshetra

    King Kuru tilled this land with penance and dharma, so it is called Dharmakshetra Kurukshetra. The Kuru dynasty began with him.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Birth of Ved Vyasa

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Yamuna

    Krishna Dvaipayana, son of Sage Parashara and Satyavati, was born on an island in the Yamuna. He later divided the Vedas and composed the Mahabharata.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Bhishma's Vow

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Hastinapur

    For his father Shantanu's happiness, Devavrata vowed lifelong celibacy and gave up the throne — from then on he was called "Bhishma".

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Birth of Shri Krishna

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Mathura

    At midnight on Bhadrapada Krishna Ashtami, Shri Krishna appeared to Devaki and Vasudeva in Kamsa's prison. Vasudeva carried him across the Yamuna to Gokul.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • The Leelas of Vrindavan

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Vrindavan

    Stealing butter, taming Kaliya, lifting Govardhan and the Maha Raas — the leelas of Braj made Kanha the darling of every heart.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Shri Krishna Ends Kamsa

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Mathura

    At the bow festival in Mathura, the young Krishna killed Kamsa and freed his parents and Ugrasena from prison.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Studies at Sandipani Ashram

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Ujjain

    In Ujjain, Krishna and Balarama learned the 64 arts from Guru Sandipani in 64 days; here Krishna became friends with Sudama.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Founding of Dwarka

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Dwarka

    To protect his people from Jarasandha's repeated attacks, Shri Krishna built the golden city of Dwarka on the seashore.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Draupadi Swayamvar

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Kampilya

    Disguised as a brahmin, Arjuna pierced the eye of the spinning fish and won Draupadi.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Indraprastha and the Rajasuya Yajna

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Indraprastha

    The Pandavas turned Khandavaprastha into Indraprastha. At Yudhishthira's Rajasuya yajna, Shri Krishna received the first worship.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • The Dice Game and Draupadi's Honour

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Hastinapur

    Through Shakuni's deceit the Pandavas lost everything. In the full assembly, Shri Krishna saved Draupadi's honour with an endless sari.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Arjuna Receives the Pashupatastra

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Indrakila

    During the exile, Arjuna did penance to Mahadev. Shiva tested him in the guise of a hunter (Kirata), then, pleased, gave him the Pashupatastra.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Shri Krishna as the Messenger of Peace

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Hastinapur

    To avert war, Shri Krishna asked for just five villages for the Pandavas. Duryodhana refused to give even land the size of a needle's point.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • The Teaching of the Bhagavad Gita

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Kurukshetra

    To Arjuna, lost in doubt, Shri Krishna taught karma, jnana and bhakti and showed his Vishvarupa. Gita Jayanti falls on Margashirsha Shukla Ekadashi.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • The Mahabharata War

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Kurukshetra

    Eighteen days, eighteen akshauhini armies. The Kauravas fell and dharma was restored.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Bhishma on the Bed of Arrows

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Kurukshetra

    Blessed to choose his time of death, Bhishma waited on a bed of arrows for Uttarayana and taught Yudhishthira dharma and the Vishnu Sahasranama.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Coronation of Yudhishthira

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Hastinapur

    After the war Yudhishthira became king and performed the Ashvamedha yajna.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • The End of the Yadavas

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Prabhas

    Cursed by sages, the Yadavas fought among themselves at Prabhas and perished.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Shri Krishna Returns to His Abode

    Ancient (Puranic age) · Prabhas (Somnath)

    After a hunter's arrow struck his foot, Shri Krishna ended his earthly leela. The spot is known today as Bhalka Tirth.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

Kali Yuga

  • Beginning of Kali Yuga

    3102 BCE · Bharatvarsha

    Kali Yuga began as Shri Krishna departed. Dharma now stands on one leg, but in this age chanting the Lord's name is held to be the greatest path.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • The Pandavas' Final Journey

    3101 BCE · Himalaya

    Handing the kingdom to Parikshit, the Pandavas walked toward the Himalaya. Only Yudhishthira reached heaven in his body.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Ved Vyasa Divides the Vedas

    3090 BCE · Badrikashram

    Vyasa divided the Veda into four — Rig, Yajur, Sama and Atharva — and gave each to a disciple.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Shukadeva Recites the Bhagavata to Parikshit

    3080 BCE · Shukratal

    Knowing he would die in seven days, King Parikshit heard the Shrimad Bhagavatam from Shukadeva on the bank of the Ganga — the origin of the Bhagavata Saptah tradition.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Janamejaya's Snake Sacrifice

    3070 BCE · Takshashila

    To avenge his father Parikshit, Janamejaya held a snake sacrifice; Sage Astika had it stopped. The Mahabharata was first recited at this sacrifice.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Suta's Katha at Naimisharanya

    3060 BCE · Naimisharanya

    At Naimisharanya, Suta told the Puranas to Shaunaka and the other rishis — most Puranas open with this very dialogue.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Shore Temple of Mahabalipuram

    700 CE · Mahabalipuram (Tamil Nadu)

    Built by the sea in the time of the Pallava king Narasimhavarman II, this stone temple is dedicated to both Shiva and Vishnu.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Kailasanathar Temple, Kanchi

    705 CE · Kanchipuram (Tamil Nadu)

    The oldest stone Shiva temple of Kanchi, built in the time of the Pallava king Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha).

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Andal — Goda Devi

    750 CE · Srivilliputhur

    The only woman among the twelve Alvar saints, Andal composed the Tiruppavai and took Lord Ranganatha as her husband.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Kailasa Temple of Ellora

    760 CE · Ellora (Maharashtra)

    In the time of the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I, the whole Kailasa temple was carved top-down out of a single rock.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Adi Shankaracharya — Advaita and the Four Maths

    788 CE · From Kaladi to Kedar

    Born in Kaladi, Kerala, Shankaracharya spread Advaita Vedanta and founded maths in the four directions of the land.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Kedarnath Dham

    800 CE · Kedarnath (Uttarakhand)

    The highest of the twelve Jyotirlingas. Tradition says the Pandavas sought Shiva here and Shankaracharya restored the temple.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Badrinath Dham

    801 CE · Badrinath (Uttarakhand)

    The abode of Lord Badrinarayan on the Alaknanda. Tradition says Shankaracharya took the murti out of Narada Kund and installed it.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Shri Venkateswara of Tirumala

    830 CE · Tirumala (Andhra Pradesh)

    Lord Venkateswara on the seven hills. The oldest inscriptions here date to around the ninth century.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Nataraja of Chidambaram

    950 CE · Chidambaram (Tamil Nadu)

    The abode of Shiva's Ananda Tandava. Chola kings covered its roof in gold; here Shiva is worshipped as the element of space (akasha).

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Brihadeeswara Temple, Thanjavur

    1010 CE · Thanjavur (Tamil Nadu)

    The Chola emperor Rajaraja I built this huge Shiva temple in 1010 CE. Its tower is among the tallest in South India.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Ramanujacharya — Vishishtadvaita

    1017 CE · Sriperumbudur

    Ramanujacharya taught Vishishtadvaita and opened the door of bhakti to all. Srirangam was his main centre.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Sun Temple of Modhera

    1026 CE · Modhera (Gujarat)

    A Sun temple built in 1026 CE under the Chalukya king Bhimadeva I, with a beautiful stepped tank, the Surya Kund, in front.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Kandariya Mahadeva, Khajuraho

    1030 CE · Khajuraho (Madhya Pradesh)

    Built under the Chandela kings, this Shiva temple is a fine example of the Nagara style, its tower rising like Mount Kailash.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Lingaraj Temple, Bhubaneswar

    1100 CE · Bhubaneswar (Odisha)

    A grand eleventh-century temple in the Kalinga style, where Harihara — Shiva and Vishnu in one form — is worshipped.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Basavanna — Vachanas and the Anubhava Mantapa

    1134 CE · Kalyana (Karnataka)

    The twelfth-century Shiva devotee Basavanna opposed caste through his Kannada vachanas and seated everyone as equals in the Anubhava Mantapa.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Shri Jagannath Temple, Puri

    1135 CE · Puri (Odisha)

    The present temple, built under the Ganga king Anantavarman Chodaganga, is one of the Char Dham. Its Rath Yatra is world-famous.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Ramanathaswamy Temple, Rameswaram

    1170 CE · Rameswaram (Tamil Nadu)

    A Jyotirlinga linked to the Shivalinga installed by Shri Ram. Its long corridors were built after the twelfth century.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Jayadeva and the Gita Govinda

    1170 CE · Kenduli / Puri

    The poet Jayadeva wrote the Gita Govinda in Sanskrit; its songs are still sung in the Jagannath temple at Puri.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Shri Vitthal of Pandharpur

    1200 CE · Pandharpur (Maharashtra)

    Vithoba stands on a brick for his devotee Pundalik. Inscriptions go back to the 12th–13th centuries; lakhs of Warkaris come on Ashadhi Ekadashi.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Madhvacharya — Dvaita Vedanta

    1238 CE · Udupi

    Madhvacharya taught Dvaita philosophy and installed the murti of Bala Krishna at Udupi, still worshipped today.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Sun Temple of Konark

    1250 CE · Konark (Odisha)

    The Ganga king Narasimhadeva I built this temple as the chariot of the Sun god, with twelve pairs of stone wheels.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Sant Namdev

    1270 CE · Pandharpur

    The Warkari saint Namdev of Maharashtra sang abhangs of devotion to Vitthal. His verses are also in the Guru Granth Sahib.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Sant Dnyaneshwar and the Dnyaneshwari

    1290 CE · Nevasa (Maharashtra)

    At sixteen, Sant Dnyaneshwar wrote the Dnyaneshwari, a Marathi commentary on the Gita, and took samadhi at Alandi.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Swami Ramananda

    1299 CE · Prayag / Kashi

    Ramananda opened Ram bhakti to all. Kabir, Ravidas and many saints are linked to his tradition.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Cave of Mata Vaishno Devi

    1300 CE · Trikuta mountain (Jammu)

    In the holy cave on Trikuta mountain, the Mother is present as three pindis — Mahakali, Mahalakshmi and Mahasaraswati.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Lalleshwari — Lal Ded

    1320 CE · Kashmir

    The Shiva yogini Lal Ded of Kashmir composed vakhs in Kashmiri that are still spoken in homes there.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Ranganatha Returns to Srirangam

    1371 CE · Srirangam (Tamil Nadu)

    Hidden during the invasions, the murti of Ranganatha came back to Srirangam around 1371 CE. It is among the largest functioning temple complexes in the world.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Mallikarjuna of Srisailam

    1400 CE · Srisailam (Andhra Pradesh)

    Both a Jyotirlinga and a Shakti Peeth (Bhramaramba). The temple was greatly expanded in the Vijayanagara period.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Annamacharya

    1408 CE · Tallapaka (Andhra Pradesh)

    A devotee of Venkateswara of Tirumala, Annamacharya composed about 32,000 sankirtanas — the grandfather of Telugu song-poetry.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Narsinh Mehta

    1414 CE · Junagadh (Gujarat)

    The first great poet of Gujarati, Narsinh Mehta wrote songs of Krishna bhakti. Gandhiji's favourite bhajan, Vaishnav Jan To, is his.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Sant Kabir

    1440 CE · Kashi

    The weaver-saint Kabir of Kashi struck at hypocrisy with his dohas and taught devotion to the formless Ram.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

  • Srimanta Sankardev

    1449 CE · Assam

    Assam's saint Sankardev joined all of Assam in Krishna bhakti through Ekasarana Naam Dharma, the Namghar, Borgeet and Ankia Naat.

    See it on the Kaal Chakra →

Frequently asked questions

What is the Hindu Kaal Chakra?

In Sanatan Dharma, time is not a straight line but a turning wheel. Creation arises, the four yugas — Satya, Treta, Dvapara and Kali — come and go, and then creation begins anew. This is the Kaal Chakra, the wheel of time. Our 3D Kaal Chakra is an interactive timeline built on this idea.

How long are the four yugas?

According to the Puranas, Satya Yuga lasts 1,728,000 years, Treta Yuga 1,296,000 years, Dvapara Yuga 864,000 years and Kali Yuga 432,000 years. Together they make one mahayuga of 4,320,000 years.

Which yuga is running now?

By the traditional reckoning we live in Kali Yuga, which began in 3102 BCE when Shri Krishna left the world. About 5,128 years have passed; we are in the first quarter of Kali Yuga.

What is the Hindu Time Machine?

It is an interactive Kaal Chakra of Sanatan Dharma — a ‘time machine’ where you spin the wheel to reach any event of any yuga, from creation to the Kalki avatar. It works on mobile too.

What are a kalpa and a manvantara?

One day of Lord Brahma is a kalpa, made of a thousand mahayugas and fourteen manvantaras. Our kalpa is the Shveta Varaha Kalpa, and the manvantara is the seventh — the Vaivasvata Manvantara.

How do I use the time machine of Sanatan Dharma?

Open the 3D Kaal Chakra, tap ‘Enter’ and spin the wheel with your finger. Every point is an event; its card shows a picture and a short introduction.

When will the Kalki avatar come?

According to the Bhagavata Purana, at the end of Kali Yuga, when dharma has almost vanished, Lord Vishnu will be born as Kalki in the village of Shambhala, in the home of Vishnuyasha, and Satya Yuga will return.

Which events are in this Kaal Chakra?

From creation and the Varaha avatar to Dhruva, the Samudra Manthan, Narasimha, Vamana, Parashurama, the whole Ramayana, the Mahabharata and the Gita, the great acharyas and saints (from Shankaracharya to Vivekananda), tirthas such as the Jyotirlingas and the Char Dham, and the prophecies of Kali Yuga and the Kalki avatar — more than 150 events, with more being added.
